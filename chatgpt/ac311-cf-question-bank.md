# AC311 Conceptual Framework — hard question bank

Scenario-based, not pure definition recall — closer to how BA202/CF theory tends to get tested. Use these as the question source instead of inventing your own.

## Enhancing qualitative characteristics

1. Two companies in the same industry both use FIFO for inventory, consistently, every year. But one values inventory at cost and the other at a weird internally-invented "adjusted cost" nobody else uses. Are both consistent? Are both comparable? Explain the gap.
2. An auditor recalculates a company's depreciation expense using the same formula and inputs the company used, and gets the same number. Is that direct or indirect verification? What would direct verification of the SAME depreciation expense look like instead (hint: there usually isn't one, which is itself the point)?
3. A company discloses a lawsuit's possible outcome only in next year's annual report, by which point the case has already settled and investors who needed to decide whether to sell their shares already did, blind. Which single qualitative characteristic did this violate, and why isn't it a relevance problem instead?
4. A 40-page note disclosure buries one material related-party transaction among 39 pages of boilerplate that applies to every company in the industry. Name the characteristic violated. Is more disclosure always better for understandability? Why or why not?

## Elements — equity, income, expense

5. A company receives a $2M capital injection from its shareholders. Is this income? Walk through why using the actual definition, not instinct.
6. A retailer sells goods on consignment to a shop. The shop hasn't sold anything yet, but has received and displayed the goods. Has income been recognized yet under the Framework's definition? What specifically is missing?
7. A company's building is destroyed in a fire and it collects a $500K insurance payout. Is this revenue or a gain? Does it matter for the equity increase, or only for classification/presentation?
8. A software company sells a 100-hour support package for $10,000 upfront. After 2 months, the customer has used 20 hours. How much revenue has been recognized under the Framework's definition of income, and why is the cash receipt itself irrelevant to that number?
9. Equity is described as a "residual" — what does that actually mean for how you'd calculate it if you only had a company's total assets ($800K) and total liabilities ($550K), with no other information at all?

## Measurement bases

10. A company bought a machine for $100,000 five years ago. Today, an identical new machine costs $140,000, and the old machine (after wear) could be sold for $60,000 in an orderly market. Under **historical cost**, what's the carrying amount (ignoring depreciation for this question)? Under **current cost**? Under **fair value**?
11. A company holds a lease liability. Under **fulfilment value**, how would you measure it, and how is that different from just using the contractual remaining payments at face value?
12. Why does historical cost NOT update for a pure increase in an asset's market value, but it DOES update when the asset is impaired? What's the conceptual difference between those two triggers?

## Financial statements + reporting entity

13. A company has a $2M net current-liability position but management says they're refinancing next month and it's practically guaranteed. Is this automatically a going-concern red flag, or does context change the analysis? Name at least 2 OTHER going-concern indicators besides a net-liability position.
14. Company A owns 100% of Company B. Company A publishes financial statements showing only its own standalone investment in B at cost, not B's individual assets/liabilities line by line. What type of financial statements is this — consolidated, unconsolidated/separate, or combined? What would change if A published B's assets and liabilities line-by-line instead?
15. Two sister companies are both owned by the same individual (not by each other), and that individual asks for one set of financial statements covering both, for a bank loan application. What's this called, and why can't you call it "consolidated"?

## Presentation + disclosure

16. A company nets its accounts receivable against a related-party payable from the same customer, without any legal right of set-off, and presents only the net figure. What principle does this violate, and why would it matter to a lender reviewing the balance sheet?
17. A gain on the revaluation of owner-occupied PPE goes to OCI. A gain on an investment property transfer FROM inventory goes to P&L. Without re-deriving each rule from scratch, what's the general pattern that tells you which gains tend to land in OCI vs P&L across the whole syllabus (tie this back to what you already know from PPE/IP)?
18. A company aggregates "other operating expenses" into one line that's actually 15% of total costs and includes three unrelated large items. Is this an aggregation problem, a classification problem, or both? Explain the difference between the two concepts using this example.
