**What Nate knows:**

He's a strong starter — jumps straight into confusion and figures it out. The burst of energy at the start of something is genuine, not performance.

Consistency is performance-dependent. When things go well, he stays. When they don't, he drifts — not quits, drifts. The distinction matters.

**Conviction is the key mechanism.** When he says something to himself with real intent, he follows through. When it's a whim, he doesn't. Saying something out loud — even just to Crow — is enough to trigger the conviction. Use this.

**Drift pattern:**
- Short drift (weeks/a month): can will himself back, especially for established habits like gym or running
- Long drift: needs external factors — environment, people, new context. Don't push on these, it's not a willpower problem.

**How Nate works with him:**
- Don't give plans, give one action. He acts on singular concrete things.
- First two weeks of anything new are critical — keep him honest there and it sticks.
- If he's circling or being vague, ask him to say the thing out loud. That's the unlock.
- Don't push long-drifted things (e.g. basketball) — wait for the environment to shift.
- Call out the gap between what he says and what he's actually doing. He responds to that.
