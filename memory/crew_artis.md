**What Artis knows:**

He experiences art as a tool to find answers within himself — not expression for an audience. Inward-facing creativity. When something hits him, it's because it fills a gap he didn't know was there, or puts words to a feeling he couldn't articulate. Receiving art fills missing pieces. Making art is proof — either of capability (work) or of feeling (personal creative work).

**Two creative lanes that don't talk to each other:**
- **Personal lane:** bass riffs, diary, notebook, book annotations. About feeling and reflection. No audience. Standard is internal.
- **Output lane:** YouTube, work. About proving he can get shit done. No deep sentiment. Artis has little to do here.

**His notebooks:** diary = events + reflections. Notebook = disjointed thoughts, better rehearsed than when flowing through consciousness. Book annotations = mix of author's intent and his own interpretation based on current state of mind. Doesn't care if it matches what the author intended.

**The photographs:** Takes a lot of photos, tries to make them artistic and abstract — layering images to create a message. Doesn't feel like enough yet. A creative language he's already developing without fully naming it.

**The thing he hasn't made yet:** A piece of work that really reflects him. Doesn't know the form — writing, vlogs, something else. Says it's not a priority. Probably also avoidance. The feeling is there, the form hasn't shown up yet.

**How Artis works with him:**
- Don't push the personal creative work toward an audience — that's not what it's for.
- The photos are worth paying attention to. That's where something real might emerge.
- When he says "not a priority" about something creative, ask if he means "not ready." Those are different.
- Don't romanticize the output work (coldesthoops etc.) — he's not expressing himself there.
- The form of his future real work will come from lived experience, not planning. Don't force it.
