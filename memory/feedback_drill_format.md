When Korn is learning or refining a concept, deliver it as **small blocks: one open question at a time.** He answers, Crow says whether it's right, explains only where he got it wrong, then moves to the next. Do NOT lead with a complete structured explainer (tables, full decision trees, every rule at once) even when the material genuinely has that structure.

Asked for explicitly on 2026-09-07 during AC311 provisions revision, immediately after Crow delivered a full three-gate IAS 37 breakdown with tables: *"can you give the info to me in smaller blocks maybe open questions for me to answer and see if i got it and then explain if i dont go tit straight."*

**Why:** the big-block version is *readable*, so it feels understood — but he has a documented recognition-not-recall failure ([[user-patterns]]): he retains the answer, not the principle, and it only surfaces when he's forced to produce one cold. STQ01 Q17→Q18 (4 Sep) and the AC311 provisions case (7 Sep, where he stated the recognition rule correctly in the morning and failed the textbook application 90 min later) are the same failure twice in two weeks. Question-first blocks force retrieval, which is the thing that actually encodes. A wall of correct explanation lets him nod along and retain nothing.

**How to apply:**
- One question. Stop. Wait. Do not stack a second question or append the theory "in case."
- After his answer: right/wrong first, then the reasoning — and only for the part he missed.
- Build the framework *out of* his answers, naming each piece as he earns it, rather than presenting it whole and then testing.
- Sibling cases are the point, not repetition — give a case with the same principle in different clothes to test transfer, since that is precisely where he fails.
- Reference material can still be written up, but *after* the drill, as a record of what he worked out.
- Consistent with [[user-korn]] (learns from examples over principles) and his CLAUDE.md ban on long sprawling tasks when solo.
