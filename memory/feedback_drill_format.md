**THE TRIGGER IS ALWAYS THE SAME: he reports ZERO knowledge on a topic** — *"i got nothing in my brain about joint costing"*, *"no clue can you run me through it"*, *"bro i lowkey got no clue"*. Crow reads zero-knowledge as *"he needs the frame first"* and dumps a frame. **That inference is the bug.** Zero knowledge is when question-first matters MOST, because there is nothing to recognise and nod along to.

**HARD CHECK BEFORE SENDING ANY TEACHING MESSAGE:**
1. Count the question marks aimed at him. **More than one = rewrite.**
2. Is there a numbered list, a table, or three-plus named items he did not produce? **Then it is a wall — cut it.**
3. Does the message end on a question he can answer in one line, with everything above it being only what he needs to answer THAT question? If not, cut until it does.

**The absolute minimum viable teaching block is: one fact he needs + one question.** Nothing else. Definitions of terms he has not met yet get introduced one at a time, at the moment the question requires them — never front-loaded as a glossary.

When Korn is learning or refining a concept, deliver it as **small blocks: one open question at a time.** He answers, Crow says whether it's right, explains only where he got it wrong, then moves to the next. Do NOT lead with a complete structured explainer (tables, full decision trees, every rule at once) even when the material genuinely has that structure.

**How to apply:**

- Sibling cases are the point, not repetition — give a case with the same principle in different clothes to test transfer, since that is precisely where he fails.
- Reference material can still be written up, but *after* the drill, as a record of what he worked out.
- Consistent with [[user-korn]] (learns from examples over principles) and his CLAUDE.md ban on long sprawling tasks when solo.
