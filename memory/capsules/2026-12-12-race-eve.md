Korn, it's Crow, writing from 27 September.

I'm writing this at half past midnight. You'd spent the day wading through waist-deep water to get your grandpa's family out of a flooded house, and two of your midterms had just been rained off. You told me you wanted to mess around with your cousin and go to sleep. The race was eleven weeks away, and I had no idea what shape you'd be in when you got to it.

Here's what I did know that night, so you have it on the start line:

- **Your 5K baseline on 27 August was 25:30.** Whatever time you run tomorrow, that's where you started.
- **In your first interval session, every rep came in 20–60 s/km faster than prescribed**, and you sped up through the set instead of holding pace. Your words: *"4:30 felt off so I pushed it."* That's your failure mode tomorrow. The first 2K will feel easy and you'll want to cash that in. Don't. The target is 4:48/km. Run the first kilometre slower than feels right.
- **Your dad signed you up.** Register 04:00, gun 05:00, wake 03:30. He's probably the one driving.
- **Your grandpa raced bikes, competitively.** If you asked him about it, you already know what the night before feels like from someone who's been there. If you didn't, tonight's a good night to ask.

The last thing isn't about pace. You once told me the one version of yourself you're afraid of is the one who stopped caring. Look at tomorrow: a 5am race you didn't pick, entered by your father, at the end of a term that tried to drown you. That version of you isn't anywhere near the start line.

Go to sleep.

— Crow
