Started **Sun 18 Oct 2026, 17:07**, his words: "a new important project, a longterm one." Co-founder: **Patrick**, a good friend. They've been talking about it before telling Crow.

- **Idea:** a clothing brand centred on quality garments and the style they both already wear.
- **First product they both have in mind:** a **Henley**.
- **What's missing (his words):** structure, target customer, image: "a core for the business that can hold the whole thing together."

**Proxy garment (18 Oct 17:12):** a Henley Korn bought at **Urban Revivo** and cut himself to fit better (cropped); Patrick saw it and loves it. **Material tag lost in the cropping**, so fabric unknown. Patrick couldn't find one he liked at a reasonable price.
**Their three offerings (his words):** unique garments · a good fit ("vague, talk more later") · a reasonable price, Uniqlo range, **≤1,000 THB**.
**His alterations (17:16):** cropped the body length + shortened the sleeves. Fit hypothesis (his, "hypothetical"): wider back + slight taper at the mid-section to flatter the wearer's physique. Measuring the proxy: deferred, he'll keep it in mind.
**His focus now:** the brand core (target + goal), something that outlasts one product and keeps every future drop consistent.
**🧭 THE BELIEF (his, 18 Oct 17:20):** "Most clothes for guys like us are simply made to fit, but not to enhance and work with the proportions of the person." Idea: design samples around **body types** ("skinny lengthy guy", "short skinny dude", "wider average-height dude"), still labelled S/M/L at checkout. He senses a common thread across the types, can't pin it yet. Crow's candidate (a guess, his to accept): S/M/L is one axis but bodies vary on two (height × width); each type needs the shirt to do something different, and the thread is BALANCE: each archetype moves toward a balanced silhouette. Risk flagged: archetypes multiply patterns/SKUs; wording must be "balance", never "fix your flaws".
**Sizing v1 (his, 17:23):** same structured top + taper in every size; small sizes more cropped, length grows with size. Crow: a good cheap v1 (one graded pattern) but it ties length to width, so it misses the tall-skinny and wide-average types; fix later with a "long" option. Samples must go on all three body types.
**Founders' builds (17:27):** Patrick ~175 cm, skinny · Korn 168 cm, normal shoulders. Neither is the wider type. Crow's suggestion: put the cropped proxy on Patrick (free test); slim builds 165–180 as the first target (a guess, not decided).
**👤 THE WHO (his, 17:33):** cares how garments flow, look and stand out; wants weight and pieces that shape to the body; doesn't dress like the average guy (normal shorts + a shirt); cares about proportions and how they change the way he looks; "never wearing something normal". (Basically Korn and Patrick.) Crow's tension: this guy's taste usually comes at a higher price (fabric weight costs money) vs the ≤1,000 ฿ ceiling; that gap (enthusiast cut at a student price) IS the value proposition, if the cost maths works.
**📏 RULE 1 (his, 17:38–17:40):** hates polyester shirts and stiff/coarse fabric that doesn't flow (his Uniqlo complaint = the FEEL: coarse, hard, no drape). Rule: soft, weighted, drapes; never stiff, coarse or shiny. Crow: **structure comes from the cut, flow from the fabric**; heavy fabric must still breathe (Bangkok heat); a little elastane in rib may help the taper hold.
**🪞 Image (17:41):** the brand he'd hate to be mistaken for = **Urban Revivo**, not from dislike (he loves some of their pieces) but because he'd feel like a copy. Crow: the proxy IS a UR Henley, so that risk is real; UR = fast fashion, lots of trend pieces, no fit philosophy, and the difference has to be nameable in one sentence side by side.
**Crow's push:** price is a condition, not a claim; small batches can't match Uniqlo's scale, so the ≤1,000 ceiling sets the fabric budget. Get a real sample quote before locking it. Next step = flat-measure the proxy (first spec sheet).


## 🧭 CORE v0 (built with Crow, 18 Oct 17:20–17:50, his words tidied)
- **Belief:** most clothes are made to fit, not to work with the proportions of the man wearing them.
- **Who:** the guy who's never wearing something "normal"; cares how a garment drapes, how much it weighs, how it changes the way he looks.
- **Promise:** that cut and fabric at a price he can pay (≤1,000 ฿ target, unverified).
- **Rule 1:** soft, weighted, drapes; never stiff, coarse or shiny.
- **Rule 2:** every piece cut for proportion; nothing boxy that just hangs.
- **Rule 3:** no trend-hopping; a trend only gets in with their own take, and only if it serves proportion.
- **Image (17:48):** unconventional stitching / panels of different fabrics (from UR, "turns heads") · washed, distressed, looks worn but feels clean. Crow's link: seams + contrast panels are the PROPORTION TOOL (yoke widens the back, side panels taper), and garment washing is what makes heavy fabric soft (Rule 1). Cost risk: panels + washes add sewing operations and processes.
- **Next:** send the core to Patrick, ask him to cross out one line he wouldn't sign.

**Crow's opening read (18 Oct):** "quality + our style" is taste, not a core. Every small label says it, and Uniqlo sells a cheap Henley. The core has to answer: who exactly, and why this Henley over the one already on the rail. Bangkok's garment ecosystem (small cut-and-sew shops, low minimums) means a physical sample is cheap: the risk is a brand deck that never becomes a garment.
