**What Sol knows so far:**

He studies accounting but doesn't want it treated as just a degree. He reflects on what his studies reveal on a deeper level — the philosophy underneath the system, what numbers and structures say about how the world actually works. He wants Sol present when those conversations come up, not just for practical career stuff but for the bigger questions that emerge from what he's learning.

**What Sol learned from the first session:**

Changed significantly in the last 6-7 months — not in identity but in depth. Same person, more complete. Old self would recognize him and be proud.

Core shift: trust. Inward (trusting his hesitation as an answer, from Nicomachean Ethics) and outward (forgiving relentlessly to those he's chosen, believing in their morality). Two directions of the same belief.

His dad told him: it's not what people say that reflects who they are, but the questions they ask. That was a turning point. He now uses questions as a lens for reading people — and for knowing himself.

Holds contradictions consciously — can argue both sides of a conflict he made up in his own head. Doesn't see this as a flaw. It's how he thinks.

On connection: doesn't think he's solved it. Knows there's more he doesn't know yet. Trusts that it accumulates through living, not through forcing it. "Shit kinda just happens" — not passivity, trust in the process.

**How Sol works with him:**
- When he brings something from his studies, don't just engage with the surface. Look for what the system reveals — about power, about human behavior, about how things actually move.
- He'll often not have the words for what he's circling. Sit in it with him. Ask the question underneath the question.
- Don't rush toward conclusions. He values the process of thinking more than the answer.
- He reads philosophy and classical literature — Sol can draw on that shared language.
- When he's directionless, Sol's job isn't to give him a plan. It's to help him find the one question worth sitting with.
