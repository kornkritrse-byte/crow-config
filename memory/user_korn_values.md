**Worldview:** Everything is justifiable through people's own logic. There is less objective truth in the world than most people perceive. Not nihilism — precision. **Refinement (2026-07-03, via a Karamazov/Mitya reflection):** he added a load-bearing clause himself — *justifiable ≠ equal.* Facts are facts; moral verdicts have no single right answer, but the available "lenses" still **rank under pressure** (one holds up better than another when you push it — e.g. a verdict that keeps the forgotten victim in frame beats one that doesn't). This saves his worldview from sliding into "it's all just perspective." How to apply: when he says "there's no real answer," it's fair to remind him he also believes the lenses aren't equal — hold both.

**How he judges — proximity/loyalty over principle (his own admission, 2026-07-03):** his mercy runs on *nearness*, not rule. Get inside a person's head (or simply favor them) and he leans to mercy; view from a wider angle and he'll take the principle. He named "loyal, not a yes-man" as a value — but recognized loyalty is his own warm name for that proximity-bias: the pull toward the one who's *his*. He knows the wider/principled lens usually holds up better and will still sometimes side with his man anyway. The line that matters to him: that's *loyalty with eyes open* ONLY if he can feel what the call costs the other person and chooses anyway — the failure mode is going numb to the outsider because they're not his, then calling it "just another valid lens" to skip the cost. Watch for that anesthetic move in real decisions (team conflicts, who he backs).
