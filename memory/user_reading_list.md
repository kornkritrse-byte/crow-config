All read by age 19, most within 2025-2026.

**Kafka on the Shore** (Murakami) — Age 16, going abroad. Theme of self-acceptance and growth. Timed with his own displacement. First book that left a real impression.

**Norwegian Wood** (Murakami) — Read abroad. Didn't teach much, just felt right for the moment. Kept him company.

**Before the Coffee Gets Cold** — Read abroad. Same — vibes over lessons.

**Discourses** (Epictetus) — Didn't approach it correctly, didn't get the most out of it, but formed his core framework of internals vs externals from it. Foundation stuck even if the reading didn't fully land.

**The Stranger + Myth of Sisyphus** (Camus) — Too young to fully grasp. Plans to reread Myth of Sisyphus after finishing Karamazov — the two books have a conversation worth having.

**A Wild Sheep Chase** (Murakami) — Fun read, vibes only, nothing specific stuck.

**White Nights** (Dostoevsky) — Read a long time back; still owns the copy. Good read, and **the first literature he read while actively thinking about what he was reading** — the start of him as a deliberate reader. (Predates most of this list.)

**The Death of Ivan Ilyich** (Tolstoy) — First book that cracked something open. Hit emotionally before intellectually — the regret of a wasted life. Turning point in his reading.

**The Courage to Be Disliked** (Kishimi/Koga, Adlerian) — Life-changing. Task separation, detaching from others' expectations. Wishes he could have it floating in his mind more actively.

**Kokoro** (Soseki) — Read it and saw human indecisiveness — how people mean what they say in the moment and still fail it. Notes that context of his life at the time shaped what he saw in the book.

**The Picture of Dorian Gray** (Wilde) — Pulled the quote about thoughts not being your own if taken from others — living through someone else's ideas. Went past the obvious hedonism reading.

**The Alchemist** (Coelho) — Enjoyable, connected naturally with Aristotle's human flourishing themes.

**Nicomachean Ethics** (Aristotle) — Unfinished. Reading alongside The Alchemist. Theme of eudaimonia/human flourishing resonating.

**Never Let Me Go** (Ishiguro) — Didn't fully land emotionally, but pulled "art shows your soul" — which is actually the thematic hinge of the book. May need time to work on him.

**The Brothers Karamazov** (Dostoevsky) — Currently reading. **Position as of 2026-07-03 (night): Book 10, read ch. 1–2 (Kolya Krasotkin intro + minding the little kids).** ⚠️ SPOILER LINE: nothing past Book 10 ch. 2 — no Zhuchka/Perezvon thread, no Ilyusha bedside visit, no trial, no Smerdyakov reveal, no Ivan/devil (Book 11), no Epilogue stone speech. Always ask exactly where he is before discussing. Drawn to how characters are conduits for complete worldviews. Relates most to Ivan, admires Alyosha, understands Dmitri's passion partially. Finds Fyodor Pavlovich pitiful rather than hateable due to his self-awareness. The babe dream struck him hardest — theodicy/inequality + kindness; he's using these sessions to refine his thoughts as he reads and will keep coming back.

**After Dark** (Murakami) — Currently reading, in parallel with Karamazov: his **before-sleep book** ("I normally switch to After Dark before sleeping" — 2026-07-03). **Position as of 2026-07-03: chapter 15, nearly done.** ⚠️ No spoilers past ch. 15. He's deliberately pacing it — going slow, chapter by chapter, **saving the ending for a rainy night**. He waited since **November 2025** for rainy season to arrive just to *buy* this book — the whole read is season-matched by design. Don't push him to finish; the pacing is the point. Fifth Murakami on the list.
