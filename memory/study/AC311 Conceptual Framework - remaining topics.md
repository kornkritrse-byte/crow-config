# AC311 Conceptual Framework — what's left to cover

Already solid (17 Nov, drilled live): Objective of reporting · Qualitative characteristics' 2 fundamentals (relevance, faithful representation) · Elements' asset + liability definitions.

This covers everything else.

## 1. Enhancing qualitative characteristics (4 of them)

Fundamentals (relevance + faithful rep) are *must-haves*. Enhancing ones are *nice-to-haves* — they make already-relevant, already-faithful info even more useful, but can't rescue info that fails the fundamentals.

- **Comparability** ≠ **Consistency**. Consistency = using the *same method* over time or across entities (a means). Comparability = the *goal* that consistency serves — being able to identify similarities/differences between items. You can be consistent without being comparable (if your one method is bad), but consistency is the main tool for achieving comparability.
- **Verifiability** — different, knowledgeable observers could reach consensus the info is faithfully represented. Two kinds:
  - *Direct* verification: observe the thing directly (e.g. count cash on hand).
  - *Indirect* verification: check the inputs to a model/formula and recompute the output using the same method.
- **Timeliness** — info available to decision-makers before it loses capacity to influence decisions. Older info is generally less useful, though some info (trends) stays useful longer.
- **Understandability** — classifying, characterizing, and presenting info clearly and concisely. Assumes users have reasonable business/economic knowledge and review info with reasonable diligence — not a license to dumb things down, but also not an excuse to omit relevant info just because it's complex.

## 2. Elements — equity, income, expense (asset/liability already done)

- **Equity** = residual interest in the assets after deducting liabilities (Assets − Liabilities). Not independently defined — it's whatever is left over.
- **Income** = increases in assets or decreases in liabilities that result in increases in equity, OTHER than contributions from equity holders. Two sub-types:
  - *Revenue* — arises from ordinary/ongoing activities (sales, fees, interest).
  - *Gains* — other items meeting the income definition, may or may not arise from ordinary activities (e.g. gain on sale of equipment).
- **Expense** = decreases in assets or increases in liabilities that result in decreases in equity, OTHER than distributions to equity holders. Two sub-types:
  - *Expenses* — from ordinary activities.
  - *Losses* — other items meeting the definition.

**5 classic revenue-recognition trap scenarios** (mentioned in the canvas, worth re-deriving each from the income definition rather than memorizing):
1. Consignment sales — billed only when the consignee actually sells/consumes the goods, not on delivery to them.
2. Prepaid game-card / hour-based credits — revenue recognized as hours/credits are consumed, not when cash is received.
3. Construction-type long contracts — percentage-of-completion vs completed-contract logic.
4. (+2 more scenarios flagged in the canvas but not spelled out — if these come up, re-derive from "increase in assets/decrease in liabilities, not from owners" rather than guessing a rule.)

## 3. Measurement bases

- **Historical cost** — locked to the transaction-date price. Does NOT update over time, except for impairment (assets) or a liability becoming onerous.
- **Current value** bases (all update to reflect today's conditions):
  - *Fair value* — exit price, in an orderly market transaction between market participants.
  - *Value in use* (assets) / *fulfilment value* (liabilities) — present value of expected future cash flows from using/settling the item (entity-specific, not market-based).
  - *Current cost* — entry price, updated to what it would cost to acquire/replace the equivalent asset today.

The exam-relevant distinction: historical cost is backward-looking and sticky; all three current-value bases are forward- or present-looking and move with conditions.

## 4. Financial statements + reporting entity

- A full set of statements covers the **current reporting period** + **at least one prior comparative period**. Limited forward-looking info allowed, only if it ties back to existing assets/liabilities/income/expenses already recognized (not speculative forecasts).
- **Going concern** — TSA 570 indicators of doubt: net liability or current-liability position · capital deficiency · debt maturing with no realistic refinancing plan · loss of key management · critical supply shortage · stated intent to liquidate · adverse change in law · breach of a statutory requirement.
- **Entity types**:
  - *Consolidated* — parent + all subsidiaries, presented as one economic unit.
  - *Unconsolidated / separate* — parent alone, investments in subsidiaries shown at cost/equity method, not consolidated.
  - *Combined* — financial statements of two or more entities that do NOT have a parent-subsidiary relationship (e.g. commonly controlled sister companies), combined for a specific reporting purpose.

## 5. Presentation + disclosure

- **Classification** — group items by shared nature, function, or measurement basis (not arbitrarily).
- **Offsetting** — generally NOT allowed (showing assets net against liabilities, income net against expense) unless it genuinely reflects the substance of the transaction.
- **P&L vs OCI split** — which gains/losses hit profit-or-loss immediately vs get parked in other comprehensive income (ties back to the IP/PPE revaluation logic already drilled).
- **Aggregation** — enough detail to actually inform a user's decisions, but not so much extra detail that it becomes noise and buries the signal. Same judgment call disclosure always tests.
